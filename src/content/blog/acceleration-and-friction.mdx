**Acceleration** is how quickly a character reaches its top running speed; **friction** is how quickly it stops once you let go. Here that's 0.11 s to start, 0.07 s to stop and 0.06 s to shed the speed on a turn: enough ramp to give the character weight, short enough to stay precise.

| The player... | Rate | Time |
|---|---|---|
| holds a direction | accelerate | 0.11 s from 0 to 340 px/s |
| lets go | brake | 0.07 s from 340 px/s to 0 |
| pushes the other way | turn | 0.06 s to lose the speed, then 0.11 s the other way |
| is in the air | same rates, scaled | 80% to steer and turn, 40% to brake |

<h2 id="what-is-acceleration-friction">Acceleration and friction: the character is not a cursor</h2>

Speed that snaps from zero to full is exact and stiff. A ramp of a few hundredths of a second gives the character weight, as long as stopping is faster than starting and turning is faster still. In the air, braking drops to 40%, so letting go mid-jump doesn't kill the momentum you jumped with.

<h2 id="how-to-implement">How to implement acceleration and friction</h2>

1. **Tune times, not accelerations.** The rate is the top speed divided by the time: 340 px/s in 0.11 s is 3,091 px/s².
2. **Pick the rate by what the player does.** Letting go uses the brake rate, pushing against the current speed the turn rate, anything else the accelerate rate. In the air, scale them.
3. **Let the turn hand over at zero.** The turn rate applies only while the speed still points the other way, so a full reversal costs 0.06 + 0.11 = 0.17 s.
4. **Move toward the target by a fixed amount each tick.** A `lerp` closes a fixed fraction instead: it never quite arrives, and its factor changes meaning if the tick rate does.

<CodeBlock filename="addons/platformer_kit/player/player.gd">
```gdscript
var p := params
var target := input_x * p.max_speed * _walk_scale(on_floor)
# ...
# ACELERACIÓN Y FRICCIÓN: tres tasas distintas según la intención del jugador.
var rate: float
if input_x == 0:
	rate = p.max_speed / p.decel_time                 # soltar: frenar rápido
	if not on_floor:
		rate *= p.air_friction                         # ...pero conservar inercia en el aire
		# ...
elif velocity.x != 0.0 and signf(velocity.x) != float(input_x):
	rate = p.max_speed / p.turn_time                  # invertir: giro aún más rápido
	if not on_floor:
		rate *= p.air_control
else:
	rate = p.max_speed / p.accel_time                 # acelerar
	if not on_floor:
		rate *= p.air_control
	# ...
velocity.x = move_toward(velocity.x, target, rate * delta)
```
</CodeBlock>

The parameter file holds no accelerations, only times: `accel_time`, `decel_time` and `turn_time`.

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="how-fast-accelerate">How fast should a platformer character accelerate?</h3>

Fast: 0.11 s to full speed here, 0.09 s in [Celeste](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). At 60 frames per second that's under seven frames in both.

<h3 id="stop-faster">Should stopping be faster than accelerating?</h3>

On the ground, if your platforms are narrow, yes: 0.07 s to stop against 0.11 s to start. Celeste uses one rate for both and plays fine, so make it a choice on purpose.

<h3 id="air-control">What is air control in a platformer?</h3>

The fraction of the ground acceleration you keep in the air: 0.8 here, 0.65 in Celeste. Giving air braking its own number (0.4 here) lets you tune steering a jump and coasting through one separately.
