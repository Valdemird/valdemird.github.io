**Invincibility frames** (i-frames) are the window after a hit where nothing can hit you again. Here it lasts **1.2 s**, you blink the whole time, and a mistake costs one mask instead of one per tick.

<h2 id="what-are-i-frames">I-frames after a hit: one mistake, one mask</h2>

Contact damage is why they exist. An enemy's body or a damage pad is a hitbox that's always on and tries to hit you on every physics tick, 120 times a second. Without the window, standing inside an enemy for an instant would cost a mask per tick. The hit also knocks you back and takes your control for 0.2 s; the rest of the 1.2 s is time to get clear. Health is five masks, the same as [Hollow Knight](https://hollowknight.wiki.fextralife.com/Health+Mask) starts you with.

<h2 id="what-is-hazard-respawn">Hazard respawn: spikes that cost a mask, not a life</h2>

Spikes, pits and the void don't kill. They cost a mask and put you back on the last safe ground you stood on; only the last mask sends you to the checkpoint. Health is the error budget for the area, and a spike shouldn't make you replay what you already knew how to do. I-frames never protect you from spikes, or an enemy hit would buy you 1.2 s to cross a spike pit.

<h2 id="how-to-implement">How to implement i-frames and hazard respawn</h2>

1. **One invulnerability timer in the health component.** A hit that lands sets it; while it runs, every hit is refused and contact hitboxes try again next tick.
2. **Knock the player away on a hit.** If they're still inside the enemy when the timer runs out, the next hit lands on the very next tick.
3. **Let the movement code ask before it dies.** For a hazard, charge a mask and send the player to the last safe ground instead. Only record ground that's really safe: static, with floor on both sides and no hazard nearby.
4. **Make hazard hits ignore invulnerability.**

```gdscript
## Aplica un golpe. Devuelve false si no hizo efecto (ya muerto o invulnerable).
func take(hit: Hit) -> bool:
	if is_dead():
		return false
	if _invulnerable > 0.0 and not hit.ignores_invulnerability:
		return false
	current = maxi(current - hit.damage, 0)
	_invulnerable = invulnerability_time
	changed.emit(current, maximum)
	damaged.emit(hit)
	if current == 0:
		died.emit(hit)
	return true
```

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="how-long-i-frames">How long should invincibility frames last?</h3>

Long enough to get out of danger: 1.2 s after a hit here, 0.2 s of it without control. A [dodge](/blog/dodge-dash-i-frames/) is much shorter: 0.21 s.

<h3 id="spikes-kill-or-damage">Should spikes kill or take health?</h3>

If your game has health, take health. Here a spike costs one mask and returns you to the last safe ground. Only the last mask kills.
