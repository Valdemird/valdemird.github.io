A **dash** is a short burst at a fixed speed for a fixed time, with gravity switched off: 900 px/s for 0.16 s, 3.6 tiles every time, in any of eight directions. Because the distance never changes, a level can be measured against it.

<h2 id="what-is-a-dash">Dash: a fixed distance you can design around</h2>

Speed, duration and direction are fixed the instant you press, so nothing can bend the dash once it starts. A diagonal dash is normalized and covers the same 3.6 tiles along the diagonal. Jump first and dash at the top, and the reach goes from 6.4 tiles to about 9.5.

<h2 id="freeze-and-buffer">Dash freeze and buffer: what T switches off</h2>

T leaves speed, duration and distance alone and removes the two things around the dash. The freeze is a hitstop: the whole game stops for 45 ms as the dash starts, and that pause is what makes it hit. The buffer keeps a press for 100 ms while the dash isn't available, like the [jump buffer](/blog/coyote-time-jump-buffer/). Celeste freezes its dash for 50 ms.

<h2 id="how-to-implement">How to implement a dash</h2>

1. **Make the dash its own state.** While it lasts, skip the normal step, which is where gravity, air control and jumping live.
2. **Fix the direction at the press** and set the velocity again every tick. On the floor, drop the downward part.
3. **Cut the speed on the way out**: sideways to the run speed (340 px/s), upward to 40%. Without that cut the character leaves at 900 px/s and slides past the spot the level was measured for.
4. **Recharge it on the floor or a bounce**, one per flight, with a cooldown on the floor.

```gdscript
func _start_dash(on_floor: bool) -> void:
	var dir := Vector2(input_x, input_y)
	if on_floor and dir.y > 0.0:
		dir.y = 0.0
	if dir == Vector2.ZERO:
		dir = Vector2(facing, 0.0)
	dash_dir = dir.normalized()
	# ...
	velocity = dash_dir * params.dash_speed

func _step_dash() -> void:
	# Durante el dash la velocidad es constante y la gravedad no actúa:
	# la distancia recorrida es siempre la misma (predecible = diseñable).
	velocity = dash_dir * params.dash_speed

func _end_dash(boosted: bool) -> void:
	state = State.NORMAL
	# Al terminar se recorta la velocidad: el dash acaba donde el diseñador espera.
	var limit := params.max_speed * (params.dash_jump_speed_mult if boosted else 1.0)
	velocity.x = clampf(velocity.x, -limit, limit)
	if velocity.y < 0.0:
		velocity.y *= params.dash_end_up_mult
```
</CodeBlock>

`boosted` is the dash jump: jumping during a dash on the floor keeps 1.4 times the run speed.

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="dash-duration">How long should a dash last?</h3>

0.16 s here and 0.15 s in [Celeste](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs). Short enough to read as one burst, long enough to cover a few tiles.

<h3 id="gravity-during-dash">Should gravity apply during a dash?</h3>

No. Without gravity the distance is speed times time and nothing else, which is what makes it measurable. Handle the vertical after the dash: this lab keeps 40% of the upward speed, Celeste 75%.

<h3 id="dash-cooldown">How long should the dash cooldown be?</h3>

If the dash makes the player invulnerable, at least twice the protection: 0.45 s here for 0.21 s, so mashing can't become a shield. Otherwise it only limits mashing on the floor. Celeste uses 0.2 s.
