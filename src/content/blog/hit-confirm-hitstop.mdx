**Hitstop** freezes the game for **50 ms** when an attack connects. With recoil, a flash, sparks and a hit sound, it adds up to **hit confirmation**: every signal that tells the player the hit landed, because damage on its own is an invisible number.

| Event | Hitstop |
|---|---|
| Taking a hit | 110 ms |
| The combo finisher connects | 90 ms |
| An enemy dies | 80 ms |
| A normal hit | 50 ms |
| A hit blocked by a guard | 40 ms |

<h2 id="what-is-hitstop">Hitstop: a 50 ms freeze on impact</h2>

[Infil's Fighting Game Glossary](https://glossary.infil.net/?t=Hitstop) defines hitstop as an extremely brief pause "for dramatic effect": when an attack feels "beefy", this is usually why. Taking damage freezes longest, because your own mistake has to land harder than your success. Celeste's [player code](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs) calls `Celeste.Freeze(.05f)` at the start of every dash: the same 50 ms, with no attack at all.

<h2 id="hit-confirmation">Hit confirmation: every signal that says it connected</h2>

Hitstop is the time channel. The attacker also recoils at 170 px/s for 70 ms, the target flashes and gets pushed, sparks fly along the hit, and the camera and gamepad shake a little. T turns all of it off at once: same damage, nothing to tell you about it. Fighting games use [hit confirm](https://glossary.infil.net/?t=Hit+Confirm) for the player's side, seeing the first hit land before continuing the combo.

<h2 id="how-to-implement">How to implement hitstop and hit confirmation</h2>

1. **Freeze with the time scale, end it with the real clock.** At a time scale of zero the game's own timers stop too, including the one you'd use to unfreeze.
2. **Keep the longest pending freeze.** Two hits on the same tick don't add up: a slash through two targets freezes for 50 ms, not 100.
3. **Announce the hit; don't call the effects from the damage code.** Presentation, victim and attacker each react on their own, so one key can turn it all off without touching the damage.
4. **Push the attacker back.** Without recoil, a slash thrown at a run carries you into the enemy, and its contact damage punishes the hit you just landed.

```gdscript
func _enter_tree() -> void:
	# Debe seguir procesando aunque el juego esté en pausa o congelado.
	process_mode = Node.PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS

## HIT STOP: congela el juego unas centésimas para dar peso a un impacto.
## 30–80 ms bastan; más de 120 ms se siente como un tirón.
func hitstop(seconds: float) -> void:
	if not Lab.on(&"juice_hitstop") or seconds <= 0.0:
		return
	# Usamos reloj real: con time_scale = 0 los temporizadores del juego no avanzan.
	_freeze_until_msec = maxi(_freeze_until_msec, Time.get_ticks_msec() + int(seconds * 1000.0))
	_frozen = true
	Engine.time_scale = 0.0

func _process(_delta: float) -> void:
	if _frozen and Time.get_ticks_msec() >= _freeze_until_msec:
		_frozen = false
		Engine.time_scale = Lab.game_speed
```

`_process` still gets called while the game's delta is zero, so it can check the clock and end the freeze.

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="how-long-hitstop">How long should hitstop be?</h3>

About 50 ms per hit, 3 frames at 60 fps, and 90 ms for a finisher. Past 120 ms it feels like a hitch.

<h3 id="hit-confirm-vs-hitstop">Is hit confirm the same as hitstop?</h3>

No. Hitstop is one channel of it. In fighting games, hit confirm is also a player skill: noticing that a hit landed and only then continuing the combo.
