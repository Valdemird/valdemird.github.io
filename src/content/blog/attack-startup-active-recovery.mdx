Every attack has three phases: **startup** until it can hit, **active** while the hitbox is on, and **recovery** until you can act again. Here the slash lasts **35, 100 and 200 ms**, and only the middle 100 ms can hurt anything.

<h2 id="what-are-startup-active-recovery">Startup, active and recovery: what each phase decides</h2>

Startup decides how fast the button feels, active how forgiving the timing is, and recovery what a miss costs. [Infil's Fighting Game Glossary](https://glossary.infil.net/?t=Startup) calls them the three stages of an attack, and only the active frames have a hitbox. At 60 fps this slash is about 2, 6 and 12 frames.

<h2 id="attack-buffer">Attack buffer: an early press is still a press</h2>

An attack buffer remembers a press made while the character is busy for **150 ms**, and the attack comes out as soon as it can: the [jump buffer](/blog/coyote-time-jump-buffer/) on another button. In a combo, a stored press also cuts the recovery from 90 ms in, so the next hit starts at 225 ms instead of 335. Fighting games call that a [cancel](https://glossary.infil.net/?t=Cancel). Waiting out the whole recovery is a [link](https://glossary.infil.net/?t=Link), and it's all T leaves you.

<h2 id="how-to-implement">How to implement attack phases and an attack buffer</h2>

1. **One timer per swing.** Each phase compares it with its own length. The hitbox turns on when startup ends and off when active ends.
2. **The button only fills a buffer.** The swing starts when the character is idle and the buffer still has time left.
3. **In a combo, let a buffered press cut the recovery,** here from 45% of it.
4. **Never set recovery to zero, and don't make the buffer much longer than it.** Without recovery nobody thinks before pressing. With a huge buffer, a press the player gave up on still comes out.

<CodeBlock filename="addons/combat_kit/player/player_combat.gd">
```gdscript
		if Lab.on(&"attack_buffer"):
			_attack_buffer = params.attack_buffer_time
	# ...
	match phase:
		Phase.STARTUP:
			if _phase_time >= startup_time():
				_set_phase(Phase.ACTIVE)
				slash.begin()
		Phase.ACTIVE:
			# ...
			if _phase_time >= active_time() or grounded:
				slash.end()
				_set_phase(Phase.RECOVERY)
		Phase.RECOVERY:
			if _phase_time >= recovery_time():
				_end_attack()
			elif _attack_buffer > 0.0 and not is_healing and Lab.on(&"attack_buffer") \
					and _phase_time >= recovery_time() * params.combo_cancel and _chains(_decide(true)):
				_end_attack()
	# ...
	if phase == Phase.IDLE and _attack_buffer > 0.0 and not is_healing and not dashing and not spent:
		_start_attack()
```
</CodeBlock>

`slash.begin()` also empties the list of hurtboxes already hit, so each target takes one hit per activation.

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="attack-startup-length">How long should attack startup be?</h3>

For the player, as short as the animation allows: 35 ms here, about two frames at 60 fps. For enemies it's the opposite, because their startup is the warning that lets you react.

<h3 id="how-many-active-frames">How many active frames should an attack have?</h3>

Fighting games usually use 1 to 4, according to Infil's [active entry](https://glossary.infil.net/?t=Active). This slash uses 6 (100 ms), a wide window for a game where you and your targets keep moving mid-swing.
