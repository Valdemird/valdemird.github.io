A **one-way platform** is solid from above and empty from below: you jump up through it, and drop down through it on purpose. A **moving platform** carries you, and when you jump off it you keep its speed.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Drop-through window | 0.22 s |
| Fast platform | 267 px/s |
| Running jump off it | 340 + 267 = 607 px/s |
| Gap that speed clears | 8 tiles (a full running jump covers 6.4) |

<h2 id="what-is-a-one-way-platform">One-way platforms: up through, down on purpose</h2>

One-way platforms let the player climb a tower without walking around every floor. Dropping through takes down + jump, never down alone: down alone is crouch, and crouching on a one-way platform shouldn't drop you. [Celeste's code](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs) calls them jump-thrus.

<h2 id="what-is-moving-platform-momentum">Moving platform momentum: jump off with its speed</h2>

While you stand on a moving platform, it carries you and you don't slide. Jump, and you keep its speed on top of your own: run along a platform doing 267 px/s and you leave at 607 px/s, enough for an 8-tile gap that a 6.4-tile running jump can't clear. Keep its sideways and upward motion but drop the downward part, so jumping off a platform on its way down never shortens the jump.

<h2 id="how-to-implement">How to implement one-way and moving platforms</h2>

1. **One-way:** collide only from above, with some margin (6 px on an 8 px platform) so fast falls don't slip through, and put these platforms on their own collision layer.
2. **Drop-through:** on down + jump over a one-way floor, stop colliding with that layer for 0.22 s and nudge the player 2 px down. Clear the jump buffer and coyote time too, or the same press jumps you straight back up.
3. **Moving platforms:** move them in the physics step, the same step as the player, and carry whoever stands on them.
4. **Bleed extra speed slowly in the air.** If air control pulls you back to run speed at the usual rate, the inherited speed is gone in about 0.1 s and the jump falls short.

```gdscript
	set_collision_mask_value(LAYER_ONE_WAY, false)

	# INERCIA DE PLATAFORMA: al saltar desde una plataforma móvil se hereda su

			else PLATFORM_ON_LEAVE_DO_NOTHING)

	if absf(velocity.x) > p.max_speed:

```

The platform is an `AnimatableBody2D`, so `move_and_slide()` carries the rider, and `air_overspeed_decel` is 140 px/s², slow enough for the inherited speed to last the whole jump.

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="jump-falls-short">Why does my jump off a moving platform fall short?</h3>

Check your air control first: speed above the run speed should bleed off slowly in the air (140 px/s² here, against about 2,473 for normal air acceleration). Then run with the platform. Jump standing still and you leave at 267 px/s, under your own run speed, so it's an ordinary jump.

<h3 id="which-platform-on-leave">Which platform_on_leave should I use in Godot?</h3>

`PLATFORM_ON_LEAVE_ADD_UPWARD_VELOCITY`. It keeps the sideways momentum and ignores downward motion, so a jump never comes out shorter. The default, `PLATFORM_ON_LEAVE_ADD_VELOCITY`, adds the downward part too.
