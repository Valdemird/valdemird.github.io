<Callout type="important" title="The thesis">
Real gravity is the wrong default for a game. A symmetric parabola looks floaty on screen, and the fix is to stop treating the jump as physics and start treating it as two durations you choose: how long the rise takes, and how long the fall takes.
</Callout>

<h2 id="what-is-asymmetric-gravity">What is asymmetric gravity in a platformer?</h2>

Asymmetric gravity means the character falls with stronger gravity than it rises with. Real-world gravity is the same in both directions, which gives a symmetric parabola: the descent takes exactly as long as the climb. On screen that reads as floaty. Making the fall faster gives the jump weight, shortens the wait before you're back in control, and makes the landing spot easier to predict.

You don't tune it by typing gravity values. You pick a height and two times, and derive the physics from them: initial speed v₀ = 2h / t and gravity g = 2h / t². With h = 3.6 tiles (144 px), a 0.38 s rise and a 0.30 s fall, the lab gets 758 px/s of takeoff speed, 1,994 px/s² on the way up and 3,200 px/s² on the way down.

<CodeBlock filename="addons/platformer_kit/player/movement_params.gd">
```gdscript
## Velocidad inicial del salto: v0 = 2h / t
var jump_velocity: float:
	get:
		return 2.0 * jump_height * tile_size / time_to_apex

## Gravedad de subida: g = 2h / t²
var gravity_up: float:
	get:
		return 2.0 * jump_height * tile_size / (time_to_apex * time_to_apex)

## Gravedad de caída (mayor que la de subida si time_to_fall < time_to_apex).
var gravity_down: float:
	get:
		return 2.0 * jump_height * tile_size / (time_to_fall * time_to_fall)
```
</CodeBlock>

<h2 id="terminal-velocity">Terminal velocity: why the fall needs a cap</h2>

Stronger fall gravity has a side effect: long falls get very fast, very quickly. Without a cap, a drop of ten tiles gets hard to steer and hard to read. My lab caps the fall at 900 px/s, which is 22.5 tiles per second. Celeste's cap is `MaxFall = 160f` pixels per second on an 8-pixel tile grid, 20 tiles per second, and holding down while falling raises it toward `FastMaxFall = 240f`.<Sidenote>Both constants are near the top of [Player.cs](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs), the player code the Celeste team put on GitHub.</Sidenote>

<CodeBlock filename="addons/platformer_kit/player/player.gd">
```gdscript
else:
	# GRAVEDAD ASIMÉTRICA: caer más rápido de lo que se sube. Quita la
	# sensación "flotante" de la parábola simétrica y da peso al aterrizaje.
	gravity = p.gravity_down if Lab.on(&"asym_gravity") else p.gravity_up

# ...
# Velocidad terminal: siempre acotada (la de la caída rápida es otra, mayor).
var terminal := maxf(p.max_fall_speed, p.fast_fall_speed) if is_fast_falling else p.max_fall_speed
velocity.y = minf(velocity.y + gravity * delta, terminal)
```
</CodeBlock>

The 56% from the top of this post is the cap at work. More than half of my landings came from falls long enough to reach it, so the cap, not the gravity, set the impact.

<h2 id="what-is-apex-hang">What is apex hang?</h2>

Apex hang (also called apex modifiers) is a short window near the top of a jump where gravity is reduced and air control is increased, as long as you keep holding jump. In my lab it kicks in when the vertical speed is under 90 px/s: gravity drops to half and horizontal acceleration goes up by 50%.

The top of the jump is where you decide where you're going to land. Giving the player a few extra hundredths of a second, and a bit more grip, right there makes precise landings a matter of intention instead of luck. Celeste does exactly this: when the vertical speed is under `HalfGravThreshold = 40f` and jump is held, gravity is multiplied by 0.5.

<CodeBlock filename="addons/platformer_kit/player/player.gd">
```gdscript
func _at_apex(on_floor: bool) -> bool:
	return (not on_floor and _jumping and not _jump_cut
			and absf(velocity.y) < params.apex_threshold and Lab.on(&"apex_hang"))

# ÁPICE: gravedad reducida un instante en lo alto del salto (si se mantiene el botón).
if jump_held and _at_apex(on_floor):
	gravity *= p.apex_gravity_mult

# ...
if _at_apex(on_floor):
	rate *= p.apex_accel_mult                         # más control en el ápice
```
</CodeBlock>

The effect is small in numbers and easy to feel. The lab's own trajectory readout shows it: the same full jump takes 0.42 s to reach the top with apex hang and 0.38 s without. Sheet 04 is a row of one-tile pillars over spikes, which is where those 0.04 s pay off.

<Collapsible title="How Celeste gets a good jump with a single gravity">
Celeste never switches to a separate fall gravity. Its `Gravity = 900f` is the same in both directions. The shape of its jump comes from the two other pieces: the variable-jump timer that holds the takeoff speed for up to 0.2 s while you hold the button (see [variable jump height](/blog/variable-jump-height/)), and the half gravity at the apex:

<CodeBlock filename="Celeste/Source/Player/Player.cs">
```csharp
float mult = (Math.Abs(Speed.Y) < HalfGravThreshold && (Input.Jump.Check || AutoJump)) ? .5f : 1f;
// ...
Speed.Y = Calc.Approach(Speed.Y, max, Gravity * mult * Engine.DeltaTime);
```
</CodeBlock>

Holding at constant speed on the way up and then halving gravity at the top already gives a fast climb and a lingering apex. My lab gets there with three knobs instead of two: rise time, fall time and apex hang. Neither approach is the right one in general. They're two ways of saying the same thing: a game jump is designed, not simulated.
</Collapsible>

<h2 id="when-to-use">When to use each one</h2>

| Situation | Asymmetric gravity | Apex hang |
|---|---|---|
| Fast action platformers | Yes | Yes |
| Precision landings on small platforms | Yes | Yes, it's the point |
| Long vertical drops | Yes, with a terminal velocity cap | Doesn't apply |
| Floaty on purpose (underwater, low-gravity levels) | Turn it down | Optional |
| A hang that's too strong | | Careful: past a point the character stops jumping and starts floating |

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="why-fall-faster">Why do platformer characters fall faster than they rise?</h3>

Because a symmetric arc reads as floaty on screen. A faster fall gives the jump weight and shortens the time the player is waiting to land. In my lab the rise takes 0.38 s and the fall 0.30 s.

<h3 id="jump-gravity-formula">How do I compute jump gravity from height and time?</h3>

Choose the height h and the time to the top t. Then the takeoff speed is v₀ = 2h / t and the gravity is g = 2h / t². Use a second, shorter time for the fall to get the fall gravity.

<h3 id="terminal-velocity-value">What terminal velocity should a platformer use?</h3>

Celeste uses 20 tiles per second and my lab 22.5, so somewhere around there is a reasonable place to start: fast enough to feel the drop, slow enough to steer.

<h3 id="apex-hang-values">What values does apex hang use?</h3>

My lab: under 90 px/s of vertical speed with jump held, gravity ×0.5 and air acceleration ×1.5. Celeste: under 40 px/s, gravity ×0.5.

Open sheet 03, switch the technique off and jump once. It will feel like the gravity got weaker. It didn't: going up is exactly the same. Only the way down changed, and that's what your hands remember.
