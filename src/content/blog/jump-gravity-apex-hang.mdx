**Asymmetric gravity** makes a jump fall faster than it rises: 0.38 s up, 0.30 s down. **Apex hang** halves gravity near the top while you hold jump. One gives the jump weight, the other a moment to aim the landing.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Rise of a 3.6-tile jump | 0.38 s |
| Fall | 0.30 s |
| Gravity up / down | 1,994 / 3,200 px/s² |
| Terminal velocity | 900 px/s |
| Apex hang, under 90 px/s | gravity ×0.5, air acceleration ×1.5 |

<h2 id="what-is-asymmetric-gravity">Asymmetric gravity: fall faster than you rise</h2>

Real gravity is the same both ways, so the fall takes as long as the climb, and on screen that symmetric arc reads as floaty. A faster fall hands control back sooner and makes the landing spot easier to predict. It needs a cap: without one, a ten-tile drop gets hard to steer and to read.

<h2 id="what-is-apex-hang">Apex hang: a little more air at the top</h2>

The top of the jump is where you decide where to land. Apex hang gives you a few extra hundredths of a second and a bit more grip right there: the same full jump reaches the top in 0.42 s instead of 0.38 s. [Celeste](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/master/Source/Player/Player.cs) does it too, halving gravity under 40 px/s while jump is held.

<h2 id="how-to-implement">How to implement jump gravity and apex hang</h2>

1. **Pick times, not gravity.** Choose the height and the rise and fall times, and derive both gravities from them.
2. **Switch gravity by direction**, and clamp the fall at the terminal velocity.
3. **Add the hang.** With jump held and vertical speed under the threshold, halve gravity and raise air acceleration by 50%.
4. **Keep the hang small.** Past a point the character stops jumping and starts floating.

```gdscript
## Gravedad de subida: g = 2h / t²
var gravity_up: float:
	get:
		return 2.0 * jump_height * tile_size / (time_to_apex * time_to_apex)

## Gravedad de caída (mayor que la de subida si time_to_fall < time_to_apex).
var gravity_down: float:
	get:
		return 2.0 * jump_height * tile_size / (time_to_fall * time_to_fall)
```

<h2 id="quick-answers">Quick answers</h2>

<h3 id="why-fall-faster">Why do platformer characters fall faster than they rise?</h3>

A symmetric arc reads as floaty on screen. A faster fall gives the jump weight and shortens the wait to land.

<h3 id="jump-gravity-formula">How do I compute jump gravity from height and time?</h3>

With height h and time to the top t: takeoff speed v₀ = 2h / t, gravity g = 2h / t². The fall time in the same formula gives the fall gravity.

<h3 id="terminal-velocity-value">What terminal velocity should a platformer use?</h3>

Celeste uses 20 tiles per second, this lab 22.5: fast enough to feel the drop, slow enough to steer.
